Animate specials with correct sequences, activated when the last card is drawn from a deck. Play special move animations in order while highlighting the active lane special, and show a shuffle-back effect at the end before starting the next players / enemy turn.

Refined design:

Core concept: timeline, not snapshots. The server executes the whole special chain synchronously today and the client only sees before/after. Instead, the server records what happened as a flat, ordered timeline of steps and hands it to the client through the existing actionPlays/loading pipeline. The draw that empties the deck is marked sequence-starting, so the client knows a timeline follows rather than a plain draw. The client gets a sequential player: step 2 starts only when step 1 has finished (today everything marked .loading plays at once under one redirect timer).

- Step model: each step = { order, kind, affected cards (with source/destination as today), optional lane/banner target }. Cards within one step move together as a staggered wave; steps play strictly in order. Four kinds: special-trigger (banner + highlight, no card movement), card-effect (cards fly, flip, or gain a trust lock/glow), participant-effect (defeat or flee: that board dims and a banner says what happened), shuffle-back (wave back to the deck).
- Per-special vignettes and banners: reuse the existing sentence-style messages from views.py (currently print statements in intSpecial/resSpecial/visSpecial/spdSpecial) - do not invent short names. int: "{player} enacts master plan (max {count} cards)" = the random hand cards fly to their lanes and flip face-up. res: "{player} holds and trusts up to {count} cards" = affected cards gain a trust lock/glow one by one. vis: attack flash on the target board, resolve card locks, defeated side dims; the banner reflects the outcome - "{player} attacks and defeats {opponent} ({vis} > {opponentRes})", "{player} attacks {opponent} unsuccesfully ({vis} <= {opponentRes})", or "{player} drains the last of their resolve and is defeated" when there is no resolve card to trust. spd: the banner reflects the branch taken - "{player} rushes down {opponent}" when power (vis+res) is at least the opponent's (rush forward), otherwise "{player} flees from {opponent} and steals a card" (flee off the board edge), or "{player} fails to flee from {opponent}" when speed falls short.
- Flattening solves the board jump: spdSpecial can fire the opponent's specials, but the server records the recursion as one flat ordered timeline. The client never knows about nesting - banner and highlight simply move to the enemy board for the steps that target it, and come back after. No depth cap needed.
- Beat structure (player draw): last card flies from deck to hand (existing draw animation) -> short beat so the empty deck registers -> special-trigger banner + highlight -> effect steps in order -> shuffle-back wave -> deck shuffle wiggle -> the existing phase chain continues (redirect / nextUrl, or /result/ when the game ended - deferred-result-redirect already handles that).
- Same player for enemy specials: the enemy-phase render feeds its timeline through the same actionPlays path, so bot sequences animate identically.
- Shuffle-back content is bigger than it sounds: shuffleBoard returns the whole hand AND all untrusted face-up board cards to the deck; trusted cards stay, and face-down board cards also stay (they are not revealed, so they are not swept). The wave should read as "everything untrusted and face-up goes back" - this is correct behavior, not a bug, and the animation is what makes it legible. The pressure is intentional game design: it pushes players to prioritize Intelligence.
- Highlight + banner semantics: WHY first, WHAT second. On the trigger step the banner names the special and the winning stat, and the revealed cards of that stat glow (they caused it). On effect steps, the lanes where cards land glow.
- Pacing: fixed total budget for the whole sequence; per-card stagger compresses as a step grows so a huge shuffle-back never drags; the banner holds long enough to read.
- Timeline construction is pure server-side data and unit-testable: a last-card draw produces the expected ordered step list. The visual sequencing itself needs no harness.
