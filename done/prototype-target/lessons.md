# prototype-target lessons

## 2026-11-18 — card_token concealment reversed: the enemy markup asymmetry was the real defect

- The `board-card-presentation` goal concealed enemy-side card ids behind a hash-token indirection (`card_tokens.py`, a Jinja global, a per-request token index, and a timeline-step rewrite) instead of fixing the cause: the enemy deck/hand rendered per-card `data-card-id` plus source attributes that the player's own deck/hand never render (`{% for _ in ownDeckStack %}` renders bare backs). Reversed by making enemy state markup match the player board — bare face-down `<li>`s in the deck stack, `data-source-lane`/`data-source-ordinal` kept only on the enemy hand where draw flights need them — and deleting the whole token layer; staged-play cookies are keyed by plain card id.
- Cross-game/foreign-cookie staleness was never a real threat: drag-drop cookies are path-scoped per board URL (`cardDragDrop.js` sets `path=`), and `consume_staged_plays` re-validates game and owner with a DB filter regardless of the cookie name. Its test pinned a non-problem and was removed.
- Raw `GameCard` ids on the viewing player's own cards and on face-up cards are harmless: a face-up card's title is already printed in the markup, and the only id consumer on enemy boards is `_animateCardEffect` matching face-up timeline targets. Hidden-information surfaces are the enemy deck stack and face-down enemy hand — those carry no identifiers now, pinned by `test_enemy_deck_and_hand_have_no_interactive_hooks`.
- When a fix targets a symptom (ids visible in markup) rather than the cause (one region carrying data its working equivalent never carries), diff the broken region against the equivalent region that already works and copy that — before designing an alias layer. Simplicity beats a concealment mechanism, and acceptance criteria must name a concrete user-visible problem, not an invented hardening property.
